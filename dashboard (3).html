💡 Digital payments grew 144×. Informal employment moved 0.9pp.
Digital payment volume: ₨905B (2015) → ₨130,657B (2025). Over the same period, non-agricultural informal employment fell from 73.0% → 72.1% (PBS LFS, 4 survey years). Digital access is widening; structural formalization is not.
💡 96M digital finance users. 5.2M tax filers. Only 5.4% overlap.
By Q3FY25: bank mobile apps 22.6M + branchless banking apps 68.5M + EMI wallets 5.3M = ~96M digital users. FBR active filers: ~5.2M. Digital identity is not converting into fiscal identity automatically.

💡 COVID-19 accelerated digital banking (2020)
The SBP Q3FY20 report explicitly states: mobile banking users grew 11.3% in a single quarter during COVID, reaching 8.19M. SBP waived fees on digital transfers, triggering an inflection point that the data clearly shows.
💡 Transaction volume growth outpaces user growth
Mobile banking transactions grew from 6.9M annually (2016) → 84.8M (2020) — a 12× increase — while users grew only 3.5×. Existing users are transacting far more frequently, indicating deepening adoption.

🔍 The Research Question
If mobile banking is an on-ramp to formal finance, wallet adoption should precede tax registration by 1–2 years. Negative lag = mobile banking leads. We test this with Pearson cross-correlation at each time offset.

💡 Correlation is strong but symmetric — directionality unclear
With full real data (2015–2025), Pearson r ≈ 0.88 at lags −2 to +2 — nearly identical across lags. This suggests strong co-movement but no clear directional lead. Both series likely respond to common drivers (GDP growth, SBP/FBR policy). A Granger test with quarterly data is needed to resolve this.
💡 89% digital by volume; 71% OTC by value
As of Q3FY25: digital channels handle 89% of retail transactions by count, but 71% of total value still flows through OTC channels (bank branches + BB agents). People pay bills digitally; large-value formal economic activity remains analog.

Known Limitations & Notes
⚠ FY17 Mobile Banking Break: SBP reclassified mobile banking users between the FY16 and FY17 PSR reports. The apparent dip from 2.36M → 1.67M is a methodology change, not a real decline. The series is consistent from FY17 onward.
⚠ Bank-App vs. Branchless Banking: "Mobile Banking Users" here = SBP PSR registered bank-app users. JazzCash/Easypaisa branchless wallets are reported separately in the SBP Branchless Banking Newsletter. The actual mobile wallet ecosystem is substantially larger.
⚠ Quarterly → Annual: Digital payment volume is annualized as Q3 value × 4. This assumes seasonality is stable, which may not hold (Q4 and FY-end quarters tend to be higher).
  • FBR filers and SME credit figures remain illustrative (pending FBR/SBP BSC data)
  • 2015 and 2021–2024 values are estimated; real values pending remaining PDFs
  • Index weights are researcher-assigned; sensitivity analysis recommended

Possible Extensions
🧮
Granger Causality Test
The cross-correlation analysis shows r ≈ 0.88 between mobile banking and tax filers, but the signal is symmetric across lags — directionality is unclear. A Granger causality test using quarterly SBP data (available from 2015) would formally test whether fintech adoption precedes tax formalization or whether both respond to common macroeconomic drivers.
📱
Full Branchless Banking Historical Series
The SBP Branchless Banking Newsletter tracks JazzCash and Easypaisa account holders back to ~2012 — predating the PSR coverage of BB users. Adding this would allow a complete BB adoption curve and sharpen the access-vs-formalization analysis across a longer horizon.
🗺️
District-Level Choropleth
PBS releases district-level microdata from the 2023 Digital Census. Mapping formalization scores geographically would reveal whether urban/rural and connectivity divides explain the regional inequality shown in the Regional Snapshot page.
